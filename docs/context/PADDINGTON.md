Skin/coat good, no ectoparasites. Neuro normal.

**Base-narrow canines** are common in Cavoodle puppies at first check (bottom canine can touch the gum). Charmaine: jaw almost always widens, baby tooth falls out, adult tooth aligns. Surgical removal extremely rare. Do not panic if a vet flags it.

Charmaine's screening philosophy for breeding dogs (context, not Paddington's own scan pack): annual physicals; radiographic knees and hips scored by a governing body; specialist echocardiograms; specialist ophthalmologist exams. Hughie has had radiographic and cardiac screening — hips, elbows, knees excellent. Addie has annual physicals; retained offspring have had radiographic screening and were good. Crossing Cavalier × Poodle is meant to improve robustness versus pure Cavalier heart/joint risk. **No breeder can promise a puppy never gets a health problem.**

Patellar luxation and hip dysplasia have genetic **and** environmental components. Charmaine's health guarantee does not cover those because the environmental piece is the owner's job.

Singapore is year-round heartworm / tick / flea country. Start a monthly combined preventive at the first SG vet visit, then repeat on a fixed calendar day.

## Joint safety (critical until ~18 months)

Joints only fuse at about 18 months. Before that the connection is ligament-heavy.

- Trim paw hair short so he does not slip on tiles or wood.
- Couch / sofa stairs. Train him not to jump down onto hard floors.
- No ball, tug, or chase games on slippery floors.
- **No shoes.** Trimmed paw hair is the solution.
- Less slipping before 18 months = better long-term alignment.

## Food and feeding (Charmaine, this puppy)

Eaten so far: **beef, lamb, kangaroo**. Beef favourite. Enjoyed lamb at dinner. Does not love kangaroo yet.

Charmaine's kennel pattern: human-grade fresh food (meat, vegetables, quinoa, pumpkin seeds — similar idea to Australian brand Lika) **plus** Royal Canin dry kibble left out as a free-choice snack. Family has also sourced Royal Canin via Pet-O in Brisbane for the Singapore setup.

Charmaine's breed note: Cavoodles are often **not** food-driven and **not** overeaters. They may eat until satisfied and stop. Leaving kibble down in the day can be fine for *this* kennel's dogs. **Remove food at night** so he produces fewer overnight stools.

First 1–2 weeks in a new home: appetite often drops because of stress. This is expected. He will not starve. Hand-feed small pieces of boiled chicken or his fresh food to bond. Boiled plain chicken is a treat / bonding tool, **not** a complete diet.

Store-bought commercial chicken can trigger skin and ear issues in some Cavoodles (hormones / additives, not "chicken" as a protein in the abstract). Organic chicken more likely fine. **No allergies observed in this litter so far.**

Plain Greek yogurt, no added sugar, is approved.

High-value training treats later: kangaroo jerky for adults; tiny puppy treats now. Praise alone can be enough.

### Frozen Kongs (family-approved)

**Savoury:** soaked puppy kibble, 1 tsp plain Greek yogurt, chia seeds, shredded boiled skinless chicken. Layer, freeze overnight.

**Sweet:** steamed pumpkin / carrot / sweet potato, puppy-safe fruit (e.g. blueberries), chia or flax, 1 tsp plain Greek yogurt. Layer, freeze overnight.

Introduce new ingredients one at a time.

## Toilet training (Charmaine method)

Metal grid over a pee pad. Grid from a hardware store (landscape / creeper-support mesh), not a pet-shop gadget. Texture consistency is the cue. Astroturf works but smells in an apartment.

Every 30 minutes to the toilet spot. No success is fine — repeat. Every hit on the spot builds the habit. Every floor accident teaches "anywhere is fine."

If he wees on the floor: paper-towel the urine and squeeze it onto the pad so the pad smells like the right place.

Watch: circling, sniffing, waking from a nap. Act immediately. No scolding. He is a baby.

Little boys lift a leg later. Not yet.

## Crate, pen, and independence

Safe pen with the crate **inside**. Crate door stays open (cable-tie or remove it). He chooses the crate. Never forced in.

Inside the crate: mum-scent / travel blanket, smell toy, comfy bed. Cover two sides for a den; leave one side open for airflow (Singapore heat). Cooling mat recommended. Air-con on.

Family crate: **Marukan medium**, sliding door. Custom cover from Spotlight / Hooga bed sheets — snug, ventilated, **no top frame**.

Day pen: open crate, food, water, toys. Night: remove food; keep water.

Place the pen in the **living room**, not the bedroom, so night waking does not become a social event.

Out of the pen whenever a human is home and can supervise. Pen is for overnight, Zoom calls, gym, groceries, dinner out.

Leave him alone for real absences from the start (a few hours) so he learns the difference between "in the next room" and "out of the flat." Cavoodles trend velcro. Independence is kindness. Puppies sleep up to 20 hours. Night light the first nights. Familiar music from Charmaine's place helps the first week.

## Grooming and coat

Fleece coat, low-shedding **relative to** a Cavalier, **not** "non-shedding" or "hypoallergenic" as a promise. Coat transition ~6–12 months (from 26 Dec 2026) through as late as ~18 months can look like extra shed and is peak matting season.

Doghouse coat words: wool / fleece / shaggy. His is **fleece**. All Doghouse puppies have at least one furnishings gene (beard, moustache, teddy face).

Singapore heat: keep the body shorter, teddy-bear face. Professional groom about every 6–8 weeks (Doghouse general) or 4–6 weeks once adult (handbook adult line). Home brush most days. Friction zones: behind ears, under collar / harness, armpits, trousers, paws.

Daily micro-sessions beat monthly battles: 1 minute of teeth, ears, nails, paws. Charmaine supplied a toothbrush. Monday is the family's grooming-habit day.

Trim paw hair for grip. Do not put shoes on him.
